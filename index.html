Research Vision

Toward Real-Time Human Digital Twins

"Bringing musculoskeletal analysis out of the lab
and into everyday settings."

My research aims to build a real-time, markerless OpenSim pipeline that transforms ordinary monocular camera input into physically grounded biomechanical representations. The pipeline connects video-based human mesh recovery, a SMPL-to-OpenSim bridge, and real-time musculoskeletal analysis — enabling downstream applications in movement assessment, AI coaching, and rehabilitation.

Research Papers

EulerCoach: Real-Time Muscle Compensation Detection with a Single RGB Camera via Inverse-Kinematics Features
Under Review
ACM Multimedia 2026
Wanting Sun, Yuke Hu, Huitong Jin, Chun Yang, Fuchen Ma
Developed a real-time system for detecting exercise compensation patterns from a single RGB camera using inverse-kinematics-based motion features. The system targets common compensations such as knee valgus, excessive trunk lean, and shoulder shrug, and aims to provide lightweight, interpretable, and scalable movement assessment for real-world settings. As first author, I led problem formulation, method design, system development, experimental evaluation, and manuscript preparation.
V-gas: Generating High Gas Consumption Inputs to Avoid Out-of-Gas Vulnerability
Published
ACM Transactions on Internet Technology (TOIT), 2023
Fuchen Ma, Meng Ren, Ying Fu, Wanting Sun, Houbing Song, Heyuan Shi, Yu Jiang, Huizhong Li
Proposed a fuzzing-based approach to generate high-gas inputs for smart contracts, improving gas-limit estimation and detecting out-of-gas vulnerabilities. The method exposed 25 previously unknown vulnerabilities.
